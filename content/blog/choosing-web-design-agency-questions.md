Most bad web design experiences aren't the result of bad luck — they're the result of not asking a few specific questions upfront that would have surfaced the problem before any money changed hands. Here's what actually matters to ask.

This post assumes you already have a shortlist and are talking to agencies directly. If you're not there yet, start with [where to hire a web designer](/blog/where-to-hire-web-designer) to find candidates, and [how to pick the right web designer](/blog/how-to-pick-right-web-designer) to narrow them down.

## "What exactly happens after I pay?"

Vague answers here ("we'll get started right away!") are a warning sign. A clear answer names an actual first step — a discovery call, a content questionnaire, a kickoff form — with a specific timeframe attached to it.

## "Who owns the website once it's built?"

This is the single most important question that gets skipped. Some agencies build on proprietary platforms you can't leave without starting over, or retain login/hosting control indefinitely. You want a straight answer: after launch, do you fully own the code, the domain, and the hosting account, or does the agency retain control?

## "What's the realistic timeline, and what does it depend on?"

Any agency can quote a fast number — what matters is whether they explain what that timeline assumes from you (content readiness, feedback turnaround) and what happens if those assumptions don't hold. A timeline with no dependencies named is usually a marketing number, not a plan. (We broke down what a realistic schedule looks like in [how long it should take to launch a business website](/blog/how-long-website-launch-timeline).)

## "What happens after launch?"

A website isn't a one-time deliverable — bugs surface, small changes come up, and content needs updating. Ask specifically: is there a maintenance period included, what does it cover, and what does support cost once that period ends? Agencies that go quiet after the final invoice are extremely common — ask this before you're in that position, not after. For what a maintenance period should actually cover, see [what needs updating after launch](/blog/website-maintenance-what-needs-updating).

## "Can I see real, live examples of your work?"

Portfolio screenshots are easy to cherry-pick or fake. Ask for live URLs you can actually visit, ideally for businesses similar to yours in size or industry — and check that those sites actually load fast and work properly on mobile, since that's a direct preview of what you'll get.

To hold ourselves to the same standard: our most recent build, [Master Salon](https://www.master-salon.com/) in Bengaluru, is live right now — open it on your phone and judge the speed and layout yourself. We wrote up [what went into it](/blog/master-salon-bengaluru-website-launch), and the rest of our live work is on [our portfolio](/#portfolio).

## "What's included in the price, and what isn't?"

Domain and hosting costs, extra pages beyond an initial page count, revisions beyond an initial round — these are the common places quotes turn out to be less complete than they first appeared. Get this in writing before you commit, not as a surprise mid-project. If you're not sure what a fair quote looks like in the first place, our breakdown of [how much a website costs in India in 2026](/blog/website-cost-india-2026) gives you a baseline to compare against.

## Red flags worth taking seriously

- Pressure to decide or pay immediately, without time to think it over.
- No clear answer on ownership of the finished site.
- Pricing that seems too good to be true relative to competitors — it usually is, in one of the ways above.
- No process at all — just "send us your info and we'll build it."
