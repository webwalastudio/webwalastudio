We just launched the new website for [Master Salon](https://www.master-salon.com/), a hair artistry and aesthetic wellness studio in Sahakara Nagar, North Bangalore, led by owner and master stylist Murshad Ali. It's live now, and it's a good example of what we mean when we say a salon website should sell the work, not just list a phone number.

## What the site had to do

Master Salon runs a full menu — men's and women's hair care, colour transformations, keratin therapy, hair spa, massage, pedicure and manicure, facials, waxing, and threading — across two locations serving Sahakara Nagar and North Bangalore. A menu that wide is easy to bury in a PDF or an Instagram highlight. The brief was to make it scannable, sell the colour work specifically (their strongest differentiator), and route every visitor toward a WhatsApp booking instead of a form nobody fills out.

## What we built

- **A structured service menu** — every category (hair, colour, spa, massage, facials, threading) broken into its own card, priced and grouped so a visitor can find "Balayage" or "Keratin Treatment" in seconds instead of scrolling a wall of text.
- **A colour-transformation showcase** — before-and-after pairs and dedicated cards for Global Hair Color, Highlights & Fashion Shades, and Balayage & Ombre, since that's the work that actually gets a salon booked on Instagram-sourced trust.
- **Owner presence, not just branding** — a profile section for Murshad Ali with his specialties (hair styling, colour specialist, client care), because clients booking a stylist want to know who's doing the work.
- **Reviews and live social proof** — a 4.9 Google rating badge up top, verified client testimonials, and a live Instagram feed, so trust signals sit above the fold instead of requiring a click-through.
- **WhatsApp-first booking** — every service card, colour card, and the header CTA all route to WhatsApp, matching how their clients actually reach out, instead of forcing a phone call or a form.
- **Location and hours clarity** — flagship address, salon hours, and an embedded map front and center for a business that lives on local footfall.

## Why this matters for salons specifically

The pattern we see with salon and spa clients is consistent: the booking channel that actually converts is WhatsApp, not a contact form, and the content that actually converts is the colour and styling *work* — not a paragraph about "passion for beauty." A site that makes the portfolio the hero and reduces every path to a single WhatsApp tap tends to outperform a generic template built around the wrong assumptions. We wrote more about the booking-channel piece specifically in [why salons need real online booking, not just a WhatsApp number](/blog/salon-spa-website-online-booking) — Master Salon's site takes the same principle and applies it end to end.
